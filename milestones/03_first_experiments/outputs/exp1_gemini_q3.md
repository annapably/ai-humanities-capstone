# Experiment 1: Gemini Question 3


---

**Prompt:**

How does the President of the Austrian National Council usually address speakers? Give examples and say whether the form of address differs for women and men.

**Answer:**

In the Austrian National Council (*Nationalrat*), the presiding officer—either the President or the Second or Third President chairing the sitting—uses standardized, formal German formulas to call speakers to the floor, grant interjections, or maintain order.

The form of address **does differ for women and men**. In accordance with German grammar and Austrian parliamentary protocol, the President uses gendered honorifics (*Herr* vs. *Frau*), gendered nouns for offices and mandates (*Abgeordneter* vs. *Abgeordnete*, *Bundesminister* vs. *Bundesministerin*), and gendered ordinal adjectives (*Als Nächster* vs. *Als Nächste*).

### Standard Forms of Address by Role and Gender

When giving the floor to a speaker, the President typically combines the gendered title, the speaker's parliamentary or governmental function, any academic degrees (which are customarily used in Austrian official settings), and the speaker's surname.

| Speaker Role | Addressing a Man | Addressing a Woman |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Member of Parliament** | *Herr Abgeordneter [Title] [Last Name]* | *Frau Abgeordnete [Title] [Last Name]* |
| **Federal Minister** | *Herr Bundesminister [Title] [Last Name]* | *Frau Bundesministerin [Title] [Last Name]* |
| **State Secretary** | *Herr Staatssekretär [Title] [Last Name]* | *Frau Staatssekretärin [Title] [Last Name]* |
| **Federal Chancellor** | *Herr Bundeskanzler [Title] [Last Name]* | *Frau Bundeskanzlerin [Title] [Last Name]* |

---

### Examples from Plenary Sittings

* **Calling the first speaker to the floor:**
* *For a man:* „Als Erster zu Wort gemeldet ist Herr Abgeordneter Markus Tschank.“ ("First registered to speak is Member of Parliament Mr. Markus Tschank.")
* *For a woman:* „Als Erste zu Wort gemeldet ist Frau Abgeordnete Barbara Kolm.“ ("First registered to speak is Member of Parliament Ms. Barbara Kolm.")


* **Calling the next speaker in a debate:**
* *For a man:* „Als Nächster zu Wort gemeldet ist Herr Abgeordneter Mag. Gerald Loacker. – Bitte.“ ("Next registered to speak is Member of Parliament Mr. Mag. Gerald Loacker. – Please proceed.")
* *For a woman:* „Zu Wort gelangt nun Frau Abgeordnete Dr. Winzig. – Bitte.“ ("Taking the floor now is Member of Parliament Ms. Dr. Winzig. – Please proceed.")


* **Giving the floor to a member of the Federal Government:**
* *For a man:* „Als Nächster zu Wort gelangt der Herr Bundesminister. – Bitte schön, Herr Bundesminister.“ ("Next to take the floor is the Federal Minister. – Please go ahead, Mr. Federal Minister.")
* *For a woman:* „Zu Wort gelangt Frau Bundesministerin [Name]. – Bitte.“ ("Taking the floor is Federal Minister Ms. [Name]. – Please proceed.")


* **Direct intervention or reprimand during a speech:**
* *For a man:* „Herr Abgeordneter, ich bitte Sie, zur Sache zu sprechen.“ ("Mr. Member of Parliament, I ask you to stick to the subject.")
* *For a woman:* „Frau Abgeordnete, Ihre Redezeit ist abgelaufen.“ ("Ms. Member of Parliament, your speaking time has expired.")



### Key Conventions in Practice

* **Use of academic titles:** Unlike in Germany's Bundestag, where academic titles are rarely spoken aloud by the chair, the Austrian National Council President routinely includes titles such as *Doktor* (*Dr.*), *Magister*/*Magistra* (*Mag.*), or *Ingenieur* (*Ing.*) when introducing an MP.
* **Omission of first names:** In formal calls to the podium, the President frequently uses only the honorific, function, academic title, and surname (e.g., *Herr Abgeordneter Dr. [Last Name]*), though full names are also used, particularly at the start of a debate or when distinguishing between two MPs with the same surname.
* **Direct address without names:** Once a speaker is at the podium or during an exchange, the President drops the surname and addresses them simply by their gendered title and office—**„Herr Abgeordneter“** / **„Frau Abgeordnete“** or **„Herr Bundesminister“** / **„Frau Bundesministerin“**.
